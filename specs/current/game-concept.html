Overbalancing documentation

Game concept

Owner-provided product intent. These rules describe future gameplay and are not a claim that the current application implements them.

Established intent

  • The board contains four scales or seesaws.
  • Balls have different weights and colors.
  • Start at level 1 with one color; each level adds a color, so level L has L available colors. Generated weights range from L/2 through L, never below 1. For integer weights, the lower bound is ceil(L/2).
  • Ball generation has a code-configurable joker probability, initially 5%. Jokers have two or three distinct available colors; level 1 has no jokers. At level 2, only two distinct colors are possible.
  • Show two upcoming balls above each stack and keep one held ball in the chute. The held ball moves with the chute and is replaced immediately after a drop. After a drop, the front preview above the destination stack enters the chute; its next preview shifts forward and one generated ball fills the back.
  • A lowered side has nine vertical ball positions, a balanced side eight and a raised side seven. Exceeding the available positions is Game Over, including when tilting raises an occupied stack.
  • Upper supplies sit above each stack; the chute can place a supplied ball elsewhere. Manual Lower/Higher/Weight/Color sandbox controls are removed in the level-driven view.
  • Balls occupy fixed stacks on either side of a seesaw; they do not roll. Balls may move between fields by being launched.
  • Owner examples, starting with one weight-1 ball on the left: placing weight 1 on the right balances the seesaw; adding another weight-1 ball on the right lowers that side without a launch. Placing weight 2 instead launches the left weight-1 ball one field to the right; weight 3 launches it two fields to the right.
  • Use the sum of the entire stack for the weight comparison, rather than only its top ball. When a launch occurs, only the top ball of the opposing stack flies. One field is one neighboring stack position.
  • A launch is triggered by a full reversal from one heavy side to the opposite heavy side, without passing through a separately resolved balanced state. Use the resulting difference between complete stack totals as flight distance; launch only the top ball from the now rising side toward the heavier side. Example: left 3 versus right 2 plus an arriving 2 on the right gives 3 versus 4 and launches the top left ball one field right. Becoming balanced or leaving a balanced state does not itself count as a full reversal.
  • Flights and landings enable chain reactions. Resolve direct contacts and evaluate the resulting tilted geometry before looking for horizontal matches. Dissolution has priority over compression: mark a qualifying group before attempting five-ball compression, and do not compress an ordinary group while its clear animation is pending. When there is no ordinary pending clear, compression still precedes emitted flights. Preserve full-reversal flight rules, then recheck matches after changed geometry, gravity and subsequent landings.
  • A ball flying beyond the right edge re-enters from the left, and vice versa. In split-screen play it enters the other player's board first.
  • Five vertically consecutive single-color balls of the same color compress into one single-color ball with their summed weights. Jokers never compress: any ball with two or three colors breaks the vertical compression run.
  • A horizontal row of three or more balls of the same color seeds clearing of the entire orthogonally connected group. Jokers bridge color changes: red can connect through a red/blue joker into blue and onwards through further jokers. Points equal the total weights of the removed balls multiplied by 100; each removed ball is counted once.
  • A ball may have up to three colors and acts as a joker.

Initial progression tuning

Start at level 1. Advancing from level 1 requires 1000 additional points and advancing from level 2 requires 2000. From level 3 onward, advancing from level L requires 2000 times L additional points: 6000 for 3 to 4, 8000 for 4 to 5, and so on. This owner-selected factor of two slows later progression. Keep points beyond a threshold and preserve already visible balls when the palette grows. The owner is considering starting at level 3 but has not chosen that change; the existing --level 3 option supports comparison.

Full-reversal warning

When the held ball at the selected chute position would cause a full seesaw reversal, blink the actual affected stack total. Keep the displayed total unchanged; never substitute a predicted future weight. Evaluate the warning by cloning the real landing resolver so bombs, compression and other special interactions are included without changing the live board, pending clears, score, supply or random stream.

Unresolved future rules

  • Multiplayer scheduling and opponent-first flight routing across boards.
  • Further level progression tuning and the final starting level.

Route new ideas to this capability or a new focused capability and use change proposals for implementation.
