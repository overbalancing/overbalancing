Game concept

Owner-provided product intent. These rules describe future gameplay and are not a claim that the current application implements them.

Established intent

  • The board contains four scales or seesaws.
  • Balls have different weights and colors.
  • Balls occupy fixed stacks on either side of a seesaw; they do not roll. Balls may move between fields by being launched.
  • Owner examples, starting with one weight-1 ball on the left: placing weight 1 on the right balances the seesaw; adding another weight-1 ball on the right lowers that side without a launch. Placing weight 2 instead launches the left weight-1 ball one field to the right; weight 3 launches it two fields to the right.
  • Flights and landings enable chain reactions. The general launch and reaction-resolution rules remain to be specified.
  • A ball flying beyond the right edge re-enters from the left, and vice versa. In split-screen play it enters the other player's board first.
  • Five vertically stacked balls of the same color compress into one ball.
  • A horizontal row of three or more balls of the same color clears together with balls of that same color connected horizontally and vertically.
  • A ball may have up to three colors and acts as a joker.

Unresolved before gameplay implementation

  • Mass after compression and mass distribution.
  • General launch calculation: arriving ball versus stack totals, selection of launched balls, distance for other weights, meaning of one field, and landing/reaction ordering. Do not infer a general formula from the examples.
  • Whether multicolor matching uses shared colors, a chosen color, or another rule.
  • Resolution ordering for simultaneous compression, matches and intersecting color groups.
  • Board capacity, placement constraints, scoring, failure conditions and dispenser sequencing.

The first-view feature must not silently decide these questions. Route new ideas to this capability or a new focused capability and use change proposals for implementation.
