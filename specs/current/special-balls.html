Overbalancing documentation

Special balls and board effects

Status: stars, bombs and stones are implemented and merged; utility balls are implemented and locally verified, pending platform CI and owner acceptance; hearts and black holes remain later deliveries. Core mechanics were clarified in owner answers 0016–0020; delivery is staged into usable features. See the delivery proposal and question register.

Stars and capacity rewards

  • Provide a silver star after each completed level.
  • A silver star landing on a silver star creates a weight-zero gold star. Contact through a flight also counts.
  • Three silver stars in a horizontal row clear the board and award score, but that score must not advance the level.
  • A horizontal row of at least three gold stars is consumed and permanently adds one row of capacity to every lane across subsequent levels of the current game. At most three bonus rows are available; gaining the third unlocks the achievement.
  • A star crossing a board edge becomes a bomb.

Queue level stars in the triggering lane’s reward supply without replacing the held ball or losing existing previews. The new delayed-order proposal below supersedes immediate-front delivery only under its stated provisional interpretation. Preserve displaced previews in hidden overflow. Do not treat an ordinary color match as a special-star activation without its own rule.

Bombs and stones

  • Random generation includes bombs at three percent probability. A bomb weighs 10.
  • A bomb detonates on direct vertical contact above or below any ball with positive weight, including another bomb. Zero-weight neighbors alone do not trigger it. Bomb-on-bomb contact therefore detonates both. Each explosion affects the radius-one Chebyshev neighborhood: its center and adjacent horizontal, vertical and diagonal positions.
  • Blasts trigger other bombs as a chain reaction.
  • Other special balls destroyed by a blast disappear without activating their special effects.
  • A stone weighs 5, never compresses and never matches. Only an explosion or a special effect can destroy a stone.

Use the existing removed-weight-times-100 scoring rule for bomb damage. Snapshot physical positions before applying the blast union, count each destroyed ball once and suppress activation of destroyed nonbomb specials. This carries forward ordinary scoring rather than introducing a new bomb-only points multiplier.

Hearts

  • Random generation includes red, weight-zero hearts at two percent probability. Hearts participate in ordinary matching but award no points.
  • A heart crossing a board edge becomes a bomb or a stone with equal probability.
  • Five vertically consecutive hearts become one gold-colored, weight-zero superheart.
  • Five vertically consecutive superhearts become one weight-zero superheart.
  • A superheart crossing an edge causes eight direct drops: one bomb in every lane or one stone in every lane, with equal probability.

Superhearts also participate in ordinary matching and clearing. Hearts and superhearts remain weight zero, so their removal contributes no weight points. Their special merge rules are independent of the existing prohibition on compressing ordinary multicolor jokers. Delivery of these confirmed heart mechanics follows the initial stars/bombs/stones package.

Utility rewards

  • A horizontal qualifying clear run of at least five balls rewards a row laser. Its bright white horizontal beam removes the entire physical row at the utility’s height.
  • Three adjacent removed vertical runs each at least four balls high reward a whole-column laser.
  • Row and column laser effects award points without advancing the level.
  • If all affected seesaws are balanced before the clear, award a feather. A feather changes the weight of every ball in its own stack to zero, including bombs and stones, without changing their kinds.
  • Row lasers remove their entire physical row; column lasers remove their entire own lane. Laser score does not advance the level. All utility kinds are colorless. Utility balls weigh zero and activate only when in contact with a nonzero-weight ball. An empty stack or a stack made entirely of zero-weight balls does not activate them.
  • Activation can occur on landing or through a nonzero ball on top, including flight landings.
  • A reward enters the supply of the triggering lane immediately.

Rewards are enqueued for the triggering lane, preserving the held ball and existing previews; the next scheduled ball and FIFO timing are addressed by pending question 0025. Keep trigger provenance through clear windows and gravity chains. Utility implementation is tracked in the utility change; its zero-weight contact rule does not authorize ordinary generated zero-weight balls.

Flight-earned ultimate joker and delayed rewards

Owner intent: ordinary clears caused by launched-ball landings and their cascades earn an ultimate joker that matches every ordinary color. Direct chute clears do not earn it. Ultimate jokers do not match colorless special kinds and never compress.

Questions 0023–0025 remain pending. The lead’s explicit working choices for review are weight zero, one reward per flight-caused ordinary clear phase including inherited gravity phases, and FIFO delivery A, B, S1, S2, C from held A with previews B/C. These are not confirmed owner answers. A flight joining an already qualified chute-clear phase does not retroactively change the phase’s origin. See the change proposal and the pending decisions.

Black holes

A cleared O-shaped group of at least three by three positions whose center does not clear creates black holes outside the board edges. The chute cannot select a black hole. Balls launched into them are swallowed for thirty chute throws.

A newly triggered qualifying O resets the active black-hole lifetime to thirty chute throws rather than adding another thirty. Exact ring/event geometry and precedence over ordinary wrap conversions remain later effect-specific details. Black holes are board effects, not silently selectable extra stack lanes.

Cross-cutting constraints

Preserve deterministic core behavior, owned in-transit balls, visible clear phases, checked arithmetic and transactional error handling. Dissolution takes priority over compression: ordinary marked cells stay protected until clear commitment; when no ordinary clear is pending, compression still precedes flight emission. Separate displayed score from level-eligible points so silver-star clears can award the former without the latter. Random outcomes consume injected entropy in the core, with documented event ordering. Do not add a physics engine or external dependency for these mechanics.
