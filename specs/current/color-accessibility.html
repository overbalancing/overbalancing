Color accessibility

Status: capability specification; implementation and owner acceptance tracked in the active change.

Purpose

Players must be able to distinguish ball colors when colors appear similar. Each color receives procedural symbol and pattern cues in addition to its hue. Only the independent weight appears as text inside the ball; numeric color IDs stay out of the ball UI.

Required behavior

  • Map each ColorId deterministically to a reusable symbol and pattern combination. Use concrete geometric primitives generated by the application; no external art or new dependency is required.
  • Use the same visual construction for placed balls, held and upcoming supply balls, and every moving drop or launched ball. Cues must not change while a ball moves or a level increases.
  • For two- and three-color jokers, mark each sector according to its own color identity. Sector cues must remain inside their assigned region and must not obscure the weight label.
  • Retain the independent weight label without reintroducing numeric color IDs inside balls. Symbols and patterns supplement hue and are not globally unique identifiers.
  • Keep cues visible at the supported landscape and portrait review sizes. Clear-glow animation, targeting and game-over overlays must preserve readable symbols and weight where balls remain visible.
  • Support arbitrary representable ColorId values using bounded work per visible ball or sector. Do not allocate a palette of every color in the current level. Treat u32::MAX as a valid identity input without overflow.

Limits

The procedural vocabulary is finite, so symbol and pattern combinations eventually repeat. Do not claim infinitely unique or universally distinguishable visual combinations. Internal color IDs and diagnostic logs retain exact identity; no new in-ball ID labels are introduced. This feature changes presentation and accessibility cues, not matching, joker, compression, weight, flight or scoring rules.

Verification

Check deterministic cue mapping, representative visually similar hues, repeated mappings across board/supply/transit, sector-local geometry and large color IDs. Inspect real screenshots in landscape and portrait with single-color balls, both joker sizes and a moving ball. Test results and owner acceptance remain separate evidence.
