Overpalancing documentation

Procedural color cue design

Presentation boundary

ColorId, palettes and matching semantics remain in the core without modification. The app maps a color identity to six geometric shapes: triangle, square, diamond, plus, X and star. The next six primary cues repeat these shapes with diagonal stripes, giving twelve initial shape/pattern combinations. Higher identities reuse this finite vocabulary. The mapping must depend on the identity alone, so the same color retains its cues when the level changes.

Shared construction

The app’s ball_visual module supplies one build/spawn path for colored shapes, sector-local cues and readable weight text. Map shape by index modulo six and stripe flag by floor(index divided by six) modulo two. Static ball placement and animated transit both use it. Parent transforms handle movement and pulse; deterministic cue construction avoids replacing an identity's appearance during animation.

Single-color balls use a large upright outline spanning 0.74 of the radius, with a nominal 1.7-screen-pixel stroke behind the weight plate. Joker cues use a nominal eight-screen-pixel glyph without adaptive shrinking, positioned in the outer cap of each sector. Three-color bottom-sector caps use 160- and 200-degree placement to keep their glyphs away from the central weight plate. Patterns and glyphs use contrasting strokes and reserved regions around the central text, rather than drawing indiscriminately across labels or another color's sector. The shared ball radius is capped at 46 world units and all platform, preview, pan, drop and launch coordinates use a common 96-unit row height. This keeps the larger visuals consistent with physics-free stack geometry. Exact geometry is reviewed against the real rendered result.

Bounds and identity

Choose from a finite fixed vocabulary using bounded integer operations on ColorId. Generate only the visible ball's one to three sectors, with a fixed amount of geometry per sector. Never iterate to the level number or preallocate every available color. Avoid overflowing integer arithmetic in cue selection; u32::MAX must remain a valid input.

Finite symbol/pattern combinations repeat. No finite procedural vocabulary establishes unique encodings for every possible level color. Keep exact IDs in model data and diagnostics; do not reintroduce numeric color-ID labels inside balls.

Validation

Meaningful checks cover deterministic mapping and geometry bounds, parity between static and animated construction, maximum IDs and representative differing cues for similar hues. Review a twelve-color fixture in desktop landscape and 390-pixel portrait, with two- and three-color jokers and moving frames. Check visible weight numbers, sector-local symbols and clear-animation overlays. These checks do not constitute a color-vision accessibility certification. Existing gameplay and input checks must continue to pass.
