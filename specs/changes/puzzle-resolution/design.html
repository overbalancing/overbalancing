Puzzle resolution design

Immutable match detection

Cell identities are lane and bottom-to-top index in one immutable board snapshot. Global physical row is stack index plus nine minus current lane capacity. Horizontal edges connect adjacent lanes on the same global row; vertical edges connect consecutive indices within a lane.

Detection enumerates components independently for each ColorId. A component qualifies only when it contains at least one run of three consecutive horizontal cells. All its same-color orthogonal neighbors are included. A multicolor ball may appear in several independently detected color components; the cascade then traverses orthogonal edges whenever adjacent balls share any color. A joker can therefore switch colors, including red through red/blue into blue and through blue/yellow into yellow. A cascade still requires an initial horizontal seed of three balls sharing one color. Identity union prevents duplicate scoring when the eventual resolver selects overlapping groups.

Checked scoring

Score calculation validates every requested cell, unions duplicate identities, sums weight with checked arithmetic and multiplies by the named POINTS_PER_WEIGHT constant, initially 100. It does not remove balls or partially update a score. The resolver validates the complete transaction before committing removals, compression and progression.

Progression

At selected initial level L, session score starts at zero and the next level requires 1000 times L additional points. Subsequent levels require 1000 times their current level; surplus carries forward. LEVEL_POINTS is a named constant so this initial curve can be tuned explicitly. For example, starting at one, score 3500 reaches level three with 500 toward its 3000-point requirement. Starting at three requires 3000 to reach four.

Awarding points validates total-score overflow and calculates earned levels with a bounded integer search using u128 triangular costs. At most 32 search iterations are needed over the u32 level range. Requests exceeding representable score or advancing beyond u32::MAX return an error without changing progression. This is a representation boundary, not an invented gameplay level cap.

Transactional resolution

Clone board and progression, validate the arriving ball, then append it. Freeze platform capacities during the compression and clearing phase. Scan stacks left to right and bottom to top: the first five consecutive balls sharing a nonempty palette intersection become one ball with their checked summed weight and that intersection. Restart that stack from its bottom after every compression until stable. Thus ten consecutive same-color weight-one balls become weights nine and one under this explicit deterministic scan.

After compression, detect seeded joker cascades, score the union once and remove selected balls. Vector collapse is gravity. Repeat compression and clearing until no qualifying group remains. Compare the final side balances with the pre-phase balances for all four seesaws, emitting the top rising ball for every strict reversal. Each flight retains origin index and capacity before removal for historical animation. Removal alone cannot trigger another launch.

Recheck matches under the resulting platform alignment; any newly formed group resolves through the same ordered phases. Every productive repeated phase reduces ball count by compression, clearing or an emitted flight, so stabilization uses no arbitrary iteration or game-capacity limit. Only after stabilization award total points and determine overflow. A temporary overflow saved by clearing is not game over.

Commit board and progression together only when every calculation succeeds. Invalid input, compressed u32 weight overflow or score/progression overflow leaves both unchanged. Flights own removed balls and are landed later by the application; chain landings consume no additional supply. Future supply generation uses the new level, while all seventeen existing supply balls retain their identities and weights. No new dependency or physics engine is introduced.
