Puzzle resolution design

Immutable match detection

Cell identities are lane and bottom-to-top index in one immutable board snapshot. Global physical row is stack index plus nine minus current lane capacity. Horizontal edges connect adjacent lanes on the same global row; vertical edges connect consecutive indices within a lane.

Detection enumerates components independently for each ColorId. A component qualifies only when it contains at least one run of three consecutive horizontal cells. All its same-color orthogonal neighbors are included. A multicolor ball may appear in several independently detected color components; the cascade then traverses orthogonal edges whenever adjacent balls share any color. A joker can therefore switch colors, including red through red/blue into blue and through blue/yellow into yellow. A cascade still requires an initial horizontal seed of three balls sharing one color. Identity union prevents duplicate scoring of overlapping groups.

Checked scoring

Score calculation validates every requested cell, unions duplicate identities, sums weight with checked arithmetic and multiplies by the named POINTS_PER_WEIGHT constant, initially 100. It does not remove balls or partially update a score. Each resolver phase validates its transaction before committing its board and progression changes.

Progression

At selected initial level L, session score starts at zero and the next level requires 1000 times L additional points. Subsequent levels require 1000 times their current level; surplus carries forward. LEVEL_POINTS is a named constant so this initial curve can be tuned explicitly. For example, starting at one, score 3500 reaches level three with 500 toward its 3000-point requirement. Starting at three requires 3000 to reach four.

Awarding points validates total-score overflow and calculates earned levels with a bounded integer search using u128 triangular costs. At most 32 search iterations are needed over the u32 level range. Requests exceeding representable score or advancing beyond u32::MAX return an error without changing progression. This is a representation boundary, not an invented gameplay level cap.

Transactional resolution

The animated API prepares a landing by cloning board and pending membership, validating the arriving ball and appending it. Scoring and removal are deferred to an explicit clear commit. Evaluate seesaw weights and resulting platform positions after compression and before clearing. Scan stacks left to right and bottom to top: the first five consecutive single-color balls with the same color become one ball of that color with their checked summed weight. A two- or three-color joker never compresses and breaks the run, even when it contains the surrounding color or all five jokers have identical palettes. Restart that stack from its bottom after every compression until stable. Thus ten consecutive same-color weight-one balls become weights nine and one under this explicit deterministic scan.

After compression, compare the resulting side balances with the pre-phase balances for all four seesaws and emit the top rising ball for every strict reversal. Then detect seeded joker cascades at the resulting physical platform positions and mark the union for clearing. Keep those balls visible and their points unawarded during the animation. An explicit clear commit scores the union once and removes it. Vector collapse is gravity. Repeat compression, seesaw evaluation and matching until no qualifying group remains. Each flight retains origin index and capacity before removal for historical animation. Removal alone cannot trigger another launch.

A landing on a balanced beam immediately updates its tilt before testing horizontal alignment. Three equal balls in lanes zero, one and two do not match when lane two is lowered; a fourth ball in lane three balances the second pair and aligns all four, awarding 400 points. Recheck matches after gravity under the newly evaluated platform alignment; any newly formed group resolves through the same ordered phases. Every productive repeated phase reduces ball count by compression, clearing or an emitted flight, so stabilization uses no arbitrary iteration or game-capacity limit. Each clear commit awards its points once. Determine overflow only when no marked clear remains. A temporary overflow saved by clearing is not game over.

Each prepare or clear commit is atomic. Invalid preparation leaves board and pending membership unchanged; a failing clear commit leaves board, progression and pending membership unchanged. Previously accepted landings are not undone. The synchronous compatibility API composes all phases into one atomic transaction for nonanimated callers. Compressed u32 weight and score/progression arithmetic remain checked. Flights own removed balls and are landed later by the application; chain landings consume no additional supply. Future supply generation uses the new level, while all seventeen existing supply balls retain their identities and weights. No new dependency or physics engine is introduced.

Clear animation and follow-up landings

The app owns a fixed, initially 0.45-second glow window from the first marked group. The core owns no clock. A player drop takes one sixth of a second, allowing another ball to arrive before the clear commits. Subsequent input does not indefinitely restart the existing window.

Once marked, group membership is sticky across a new tilt: an original horizontal seed is needed once, not again for every follow-up. The new ball can join through current orthogonal shared-color adjacency, including joker color switches. Four marked weight-one balls followed by a matching weight-one ball on the first lane therefore remain visible as five balls with zero points until commit, then award 500 points once. A different-color disconnected arrival is not included.

PendingClear exposes current cell identities after preparation. Compression remaps marks from its five source indices onto the merged ball and shifts higher indices; a launched top ball loses its board mark and remains owned by the emitted flight. Callers must not mutate stacks outside the phased API while marks exist. No stale index, deleted-ball snapshot or compensating score undo is used.

A clear commit atomically removes and scores the current membership, collapses stacks and prepares the next compression/tilt/match phase. If another group is marked, it receives its own animation window. Overflow loss is deferred while a marked clear can still save the board. Reset discards pending animation state together with the board and in-transit balls.
