Overbalancing documentation

Stars, bombs and stones design

Validated ball kinds

Use concrete Normal, SilverStar, GoldStar, Bomb and Stone kinds. Normal balls retain a nonempty one-to-three-color palette and positive weight. The four special kinds use an empty, nonmatching palette. Star weights are zero; bomb weight is 10 and stone weight is 5. Kind-aware constructors and landing validation preserve these invariants. Ordinary jokers still never compress.

Generation

Use one categorical percentage draw for disjoint ball categories: three percent bombs and five percent ordinary jokers when the level supports jokers. This preserves the owner’s rates over all generated balls rather than applying five percent only to the nonbomb remainder. Validate combined configured rates before consuming entropy. Per-level star rewards are separate from random generation.

Contacts and damage

Resolve direct bomb contact before normal compression or matching. Either a bomb or a positive-weight neighbor may arrive; contact immediately above or below is sufficient. A zero-weight star alone does not detonate an adjacent bomb. Bomb-on-bomb contact triggers both.

Capture physical cell positions and bomb centers before damage. Build a deterministic queue of blast centers, expand each radius-one Chebyshev neighborhood and activate each reached bomb once. Union affected identities before removal, then remap pending marks through vector collapse. Nonbomb specials destroyed by a blast do not merge, reward, expand capacity or otherwise activate. Damage uses the existing checked removed-weight-times-100 rule with each destroyed identity counted once.

Outside blast destruction, silver-on-silver vertical contact becomes one weight-zero gold star, including flight landings. Horizontal silver and gold pattern rules are kind-based and separate from ordinary color matching. A silver board clear is a special removal that suppresses destroyed-bomb activation.

Score and rewards

Track displayed score separately from level-eligible points. Ordinary eligible removals and blast damage use existing progression scoring. Silver-star board clears add displayed score without advancing the level. Zero-weight stars themselves contribute no weight points. Keep both counters checked and phase-atomic.

Each level reward carries the originating lane of the triggering clear phase. Preserve this provenance through follow-up arrivals, gravity and secondary clear phases; do not replace it with whichever lane was most recently selected in the chute. Insert the silver reward immediately at the front of that lane’s supply. Retain the held ball and both old previews, storing displaced previews in hidden overflow. Consume preserved overflow before exposing a fresh replacement; newly generated replacement balls queue behind it, so no existing or generated ball is silently lost.

Capacity and achievement

Consume each maximal horizontal run of at least three gold stars. One contiguous eight-star run is one reward; distinct qualifying rows can each grant a reward in the same resolution. Add one bonus row to every lane per rewarded run, with at most three earned rows during a game. Base heavy/balanced/light capacities 9/8/7 become 12/11/10 at the maximum. Bonus rows persist through level advances. The third successful expansion emits the achievement once; a new game resets board capacity.

Use the expanded maximum when computing physical offsets: nine plus bonus rows minus lane capacity. Never subtract a capacity above nine from an unsigned nine. Keep rendering, matching, platform heights, landing positions and historical launch snapshots consistent with the same capacity model.

Flight and presentation boundary

Detect crossing from the unwrapped route: rightward distance greater than seven minus origin, or leftward distance greater than origin. This detects full wraps without overflowing origin plus distance. Convert silver/gold stars into weight-10 bombs exactly once while retaining the already computed route. The converted ball remains owned in transit until landing; do not apply conversions per render frame.

The core emits explicit events for merges, blasts, score-only clears, expansions, achievements and supply rewards. The shared app renders distinct special kinds, effect feedback and changed capacities without copying domain formulas. Preserve fixed clear windows, sticky membership, concurrent throws, transactional failure pause and reset ownership. No new crate or physics engine is introduced.

Progression pacing and resolver preview

The first two advancement costs remain 1000 and 2000. From level three onward, use 2000 times the current level (6000, 8000 and onward). Carry surplus and keep displayed score separate from eligible progression. Use bounded integer prefix-cost arithmetic for large awards rather than a level-by-level loop.

For the selected held ball and chute lane, invoke the actual landing preparation on cloned board and actual pending state, retaining sticky membership and its compression protection. Derive predicted new or reversed tilts from real emitted-flight outcomes, including compression and bomb/star effects. Blink the live origin-stack total for each qualifying reversal, retaining its actual numeric value. The preview must not consume live randomness, alter supply, commit clears, award score or modify owned transit. Invalid previews produce no speculative warning.

Dissolution priority

After direct special contacts, evaluate tilted match geometry without first removing a launched ball. Mark qualifying ordinary groups before compression; any pending ordinary clear blocks compression through its animation. With no ordinary clear pending, compression still precedes flight emission. Then resolve actual reversals and recheck matches after changed geometry. This owner correction changes only clear-versus-compression priority and preserves final-tilt matching and ordinary compression-before-flight behavior when no clear qualifies.
