Level supply view

This view replaces the manual placement sandbox controls with a level-driven supply and a tall playfield. It follows the composition of the owner-provided Swing reference while using original primitive graphics; no reference artwork or external assets are required.

Board composition

A compact header uses roughly the top tenth of the view for level and essential status. Eight supply columns occupy a shallow band above eight stack positions, paired into four seesaws at the bottom of a tall playfield. Each supply column shows two queued preview balls. A recognizable movable chute holds exactly one ball between the supply and playfield, with its destination indicated by the chute position. Placing the held ball immediately refills the chute; the first preview ball from the current destination lane becomes the new held ball, the second preview advances, and that lane receives a new second preview. Eight plain numeric stack totals are shown directly below their individual stacks. L/R prefixes, balance-status text and the Target/Held/Weight debug summary remain removed.

The board is the main view. Remove the sandbox weight and color configuration buttons and the Lower/Higher inspection controls. Do not replace them with invented score, timer, life or failure rules from the reference screenshot.

Level configuration

A level numbered L uses exactly L distinct palette colors. Ball weights are generated within the configured range with a minimum of one. The confirmed default joker probability is five percent and is configurable. Jokers contain two or three colors from the level palette; level one has no jokers. There are two visible preview balls per lane and one held chute ball, which refills immediately after placement. After placement, the current destination lane supplies the new held ball from its first preview; its second preview advances and a new second preview is generated. Moving the chute does not replace the already held ball.

Open interaction and rules

Chute movement works with keyboard, pointer and touch. Down, Space or Enter activates Drop immediately, including during other drops, flights and clearing windows. Four quick presses can therefore produce four accepted drops unless game over or an explicit error intervenes. Backspace starts a fresh board. The visible Drop and Reset buttons are removed; no replacement touch control is introduced by this cleanup. The playfield has a common fixed ceiling. Balanced sides have eight free ball rows; the lowered side has nine and the raised side seven. Render the platform endpoints one actual ball-row below or above the balanced height, rather than showing only a shallow rotation around unchanged endpoint heights. Exceeding the applicable nine-, eight- or seven-row capacity ends the game. A persistent GAME OVER message states the overflow reason; a brief red flash may supplement the text. Further dropping and supply consumption stop, while inspection, chute movement and Reset remain available. Backspace remains the reset command. Launch rules belong to a separate feature; matching, compression and scoring are not implemented here. Retained balls must remain inspectable. Any wheel or touch-drag inspection changes the viewport only, never the stack state. Drag inspection must not also trigger a tap action. The confirmed per-side capacity and game-over rule govern overflow; the view must not silently delete balls to fit.
