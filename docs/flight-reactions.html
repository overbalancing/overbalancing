Overpalancing documentation · Level supply

Flight reactions

The level-supply view presents accepted drops, wrapped launches and chained landings with original primitive animation. The core determines game outcomes; animation presents them without changing distances, weights, colors or the order of reactions. No external art assets or new dependency are needed.

Interaction while a reaction is active

Only one reaction chain runs at a time. After a drop is accepted, the held chute ball refills immediately according to the current destination supply rule. The dropping ball remains visually separate from this newly held ball. A visible phase label identifies dropping, launching or landing, and the Drop control shows BUSY while another drop cannot start. Keyboard, pointer and touch obey the same busy guard.

Backspace and the touch/pointer Reset control remain available. Reset cancels the active reaction and clears its visual effects as well as restoring game state. Inspection may remain available without starting a second chain. A completed atomic landing can reveal a persistent GAME OVER state; Reset stays visible and reachable.

Read the flight

Show the moving ball's color identifiers and numeric weight, with a clear destination or landing cue. Retain readable two-line labels and all four seesaws at a 390-pixel portrait viewport. Status text must communicate the reaction independently of motion or color alone.

Always show the full launch distance as a number. To keep presentation bounded for enormous wrapped distances, an animation may show at most one visible lap plus the remainder. This is a visual abbreviation only: the actual launch distance, wrapped destination and reaction outcome remain unchanged. Do not label the abbreviated arc as the complete traveled distance.

Verification status

On 2026-10-20, the UX engineer inspected actual Linux drop, launch, settled and reset renders, including 390-pixel portrait and desktop views. Visual review passed: BUSY and the launch distance/destination are readable, the flying ball is distinct from the refilled held ball, the landing ring is visible, all four seesaws remain readable and Reset stays reachable. Paired edge screenshots show a ball leaving the left boundary and returning from the right. A reset screenshot shows an empty board without an active flight.

The lead separately exercised the demo through X11 keyboard input, observed a two-launch chain and a subsequent four-lane left wrap, and verified Reset during an incoming drop. These interaction results are not inferred from still images. Physical phone execution, other-platform runtime proof and customer acceptance remain pending. Matching, compression and level progression are subsequent work and are not implemented by this feature.

Try a reproducible reaction

cargo run --locked -p overpalancing-app --bin overpalancing -- --flight-demo

Press Space to start the fixture's drop and reaction chain. Backspace or Reset cancels the reaction and restores a normal empty level-five board rather than reseeding the fixture. For ordinary play with weights that can demonstrate reactions, launch with --level 3. Normal level one contains only weight-one balls and does not demonstrate the full flip reaction by itself.
