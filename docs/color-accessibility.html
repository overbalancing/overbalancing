Overpalancing documentation

Color and shape cues

Ball colors gain procedural shape and pattern cues so similar fills are easier to distinguish. Original geometry is rendered directly; no image assets, external fonts or new dependency are required. These cues supplement the fill color and do not change matching, weight or joker rules.

Stable visual vocabulary

Every palette color receives a mark, including the early colors. Use the same deterministic ColorId mapping for previews, held balls, stationary balls and balls moving through drops or flights.

Readable weights and jokers

At approximately 37-pixel ball diameter in a 390-pixel portrait view, prefer a few bold lines to intricate decoration. Single-color balls use a large outlined shape around a slim, high-contrast central weight plate. Joker sectors use fixed-size outer marks rather than shrinking them into dots. The plate masks shapes and stripes behind the weight so neither conceals the number.

Each two- or three-color joker sector gets its own color's mark and pattern, clipped to that sector. Keep the center weight readable and avoid implying that one sector represents the entire joker. The same sector assignment must remain stable while the ball moves.

Verification status

On 2026-10-20, the UX engineer reviewed actual twelve-color desktop and 390-pixel portrait renders, dropping and settled frames, and a wrapped-flight frame. The initial tiny rim marks failed recognition review; the revised large outlines and fixed-size joker marks pass at these inspected sizes. Triangle, square, diamond, plus, X and star cues are recognizable; striped variants remain distinct. Two- and three-color sectors retain separate marks, weights 12 and 125 remain readable, and the same cues appear on moving balls. This establishes visual review of the tested fixture, not universal recognizability at arbitrary sizes or levels. Phone execution, user testing with color-vision differences and customer acceptance remain separate.
